Backstory · An alternative approach to innovation

Play is the way.

Founders and innovators don't learn much sitting still. So we coach them standing up — building, drumming, arguing, pitching a plastic butterfly to the room. Play is the method, not the warm-up, and the community it makes is stickier than apple sauce.

The origin

It started with eight LEGO bricks and a room that wouldn't speak.

The first version was an experiment at someone else's event: hand every founder the same eight pieces, give them sixty seconds to build a butterfly, and see what a room does when the instruction is absurd enough that nobody can perform expertise.

It worked in the way physical things work. People stood up. People laughed at their own butterfly. Then they talked to each other for an hour without being told to network.

Every format since — the circles, the drums, the deep dives, the open hour — came from the same question: what has to happen physically to turn a group of random innovators into a genuine community?

Before Opensoch

A band that keeps re-forming.

Shez and Rish met in 2011 on opposite sides of the table — Rish was heading digital at Creative Chaos, Shez doing strategy and insights at Unilever Pakistan. They went their own ways: Shez to Singapore and a decade at Unilever, then HP; Rish to his own agency in Pakistan.

In 2017 they built Jambro, a way for musicians to find each other. It reached a community across 500 cities and epic jams from the ports of Karachi to ten thousand feet up at the Passu Cones. Then Covid stopped live music everywhere at once, and they exited reluctantly.

Opensoch is the same instinct, pointed at founders and innovators instead of musicians: put people in a room and give them something to do together.

What we believe

Four things we keep proving to ourselves.

01
Hands before slides

People remember what they made, not what they were shown.

02
Everyone speaks early

A person who hasn't spoken in the first ten minutes usually won't.

03
Low stakes, real skills

Ten seconds pitching a plastic butterfly teaches what an hour of theory can't.

04
The room is the product

We measure a session by who is still talking after it ends.
